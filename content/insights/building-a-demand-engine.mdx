# Building a Demand Engine: Positioning, Audience Research & Referrals

A "marketing plan" used to feel like a deliverable to me — a document you make so it exists. It isn't. It's an engine: a few connected parts that, when tuned, turn attention into demand on a repeatable basis. Here's how I think about wiring it.

## Start with who you are, not what you sell

Before audience, before channels, before a single ad — you have to be able to answer *why you*. I run through three questions until the answers are sharp:

- **What drives you?** The honest motive. It leaks into everything and people can feel it.
- **What makes you the better option?** And here's the trap: don't answer with nouns. "Service," "quality," "experience," "education" are not answers. The answers are the verbs underneath them — *how* do you deliver that service, *what specifically* makes the quality better, *how* does your experience help the customer? Cross out the noun, keep the mechanism.
- **What value do you actually create?** Value shows up in four forms: **monetary, functional, tangible, and emotional.** Most positioning stops at the first two. The decision usually turns on the last two.

If you can't articulate this crisply, no amount of targeting will save the campaign — you'll just spend money making a blurry message louder.

## The integrated plan, in five moves

The plan itself is simple to say and hard to do:

1. **Define and articulate the business plan** — what are we actually trying to make happen, in numbers.
2. **Identify and research the audience** — covered below; this is where most of the leverage is.
3. **Incorporate the right technologies** — the stack that lets you reach, capture, and nurture at scale.
4. **Evaluate the best next steps** — sequence, don't boil the ocean.
5. **Integrate content marketing into lead generation** — content isn't a separate hobby; it's the top of the funnel that feeds everything else.

The word that matters is *integrated*. The failure mode is running content, ads, and outreach as three disconnected hobbies. They have to feed each other: content warms the audience your ads retarget, and your outreach references both.

## Audience research is the whole game

The single most expensive mistake in marketing is not getting the audience right. And the fix is unglamorous: **do the research first.** Doing it before your competitors is itself an edge — most people skip straight to creative.

Targeting an audience really means thinking through what your audience actually *does*. Concretely, I try to answer:

- What brands are they into?
- Where do they shop?
- What are their hobbies?
- Do they read? What?
- What do they watch?

These aren't trivia. Each answer is a targeting parameter and a creative angle. You can't write a message that lands for someone whose world you haven't mapped. Research turns "cold audience" from a guess into a set of specific, reachable signals.

## Referrals: the highest-trust channel, engineered

Referrals are the cheapest, warmest demand you'll ever get — and most people leave them to chance. The lesson I keep relearning: **make referring you effortless.** A good referral system is a small kit you hand to a happy customer or partner so they can introduce you without having to think. The components:

- **Who you are** — a crisp, repeatable one-liner they can say out loud.
- **What you do** — the offer in plain language, broken into the few things you actually deliver.
- **Why you're a good fit** — the differentiators you want repeated, plus space for them to add what *they* personally valued (their words are more persuasive than yours).
- **Proof** — a profile and portfolio they can forward.
- **The next step** — exactly how the introduced person reaches you, so the warm intro doesn't die in a dead end.

The insight isn't "ask for referrals." It's that referrals are a *product* you can design — reduce the friction and the effort it takes someone to vouch for you, and they will, far more often.

## Tie it together

A demand engine is just these parts in a loop: sharp positioning so the message is true → deep audience research so it lands → integrated content and ads so attention compounds instead of leaking → and a designed referral system so your happiest customers become a channel. None of it is exotic. The edge is doing the boring research before the exciting creative, and wiring the parts together instead of running them as separate hobbies.
