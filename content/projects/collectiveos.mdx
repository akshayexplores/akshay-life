**Collective-based work model built using no-code**

I joined iCreate to contribute towards experimenting with re-defining how enterprises manage freelance work. Instead of treating freelancers as individuals, the idea was to help them **form collectives**, work as teams, and take on larger, more structured projects.

The goal was simple:

> Let experts focus on their craft and team leadership, while the platform handles everything else.

### The core idea

This was positioned as an **upgraded version of freelancing**.

- Experts in a domain could start a collective
- Collectives could recruit teammates and work together on projects
- Clients hired collectives, not individuals
- iCreate handled legal, contracts, payments, marketing, and sales

This allowed creators to work like small studios or agencies, without having to build all the overhead themselves.

### What we built

To test the concept and build a working model we leveraged multiple no-code tools **(primarily Coda.io, Typeform, and automation layers)**, to design and implement the **operating system behind this model**, often referred to internally as *CollectiveOS*.

The system connected clients, projects, collectives, finances, and tasks into one structured workflow.

### How the system worked

**1. Project intake and assignment.** All incoming project requests were collected via **Typeform**. Every request automatically appeared in a **Projects Dashboard**, from where projects could be reviewed and assigned to the right collective. Once financial details were added, the same project also appeared in a **Finance View**, keeping delivery and payments connected.

**2. Central collectives view.** A single **Collectives Page** acted as the internal command center — overview of all projects, financial visibility, list of all collectives and their status, and a safe read-only view for sharing internally. This made coordination simple without risking accidental edits.

**3. Collective workspaces.** Each collective had its own dedicated page that functioned as their workspace: landing page, assigned projects, financial visibility, shared resources, and tasks linked to active projects. When a project was assigned, all members were notified automatically and it appeared in their dashboard.

**4. Task management.** Tasks were managed at both individual and collective levels — a **"Mine" view** for each member, and a **collective view** for everything the team was working on. This kept accountability clear without adding complexity.

**5. Project cards.** Each project had its own detailed page. All client inputs from Typeform were visible in one place, and a one-click template ("Get Set Go") generated a ready-to-use task view. Everything related to that project lived inside a single card.

**6. Client view.** For transparency, a client-friendly view was created — Kanban-style task board, clear project stages, comment-only access. This allowed clients to track progress without interfering with internal execution.

### Platform roles

The system was designed around three clear user groups.

**Clients** — create and manage projects, track progress and tasks, communicate with collectives, view invoices and project status.

**Creators** — create profiles and portfolios, start or join collectives, access collective workspaces, work on assigned projects and tasks.

**Admin** — review and assign projects, manage collective requests, track progress across the platform, coordinate between clients and creators.

### Why this mattered

This system turned a complex idea into something operational.

- Freelancers could work as teams
- Collectives could scale beyond solo work
- Clients got structure and accountability
- The company could run operations without heavy custom software

The platform and work model later helped the company raise funding, validating both the **idea** and the **system design** behind it.

### Key learnings

- Freelancing works better when structured as teams, not individuals
- Clear systems enable trust between clients and creators
- No-code tools can power real business models when designed thoughtfully
- Operations design is as important as product design

This project gave me hands-on experience in **designing work systems**, not just interfaces, and translating a business idea into something teams could actually run day to day.
